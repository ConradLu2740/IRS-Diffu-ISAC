\begin{abstract}

This report describes an open-source, physics-grounded engineering system for RIS-aided Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) extended to space ISAC (ISAC-NTN). It combines real LEO orbit propagation (SGP4), dynamic RIS phase tracking, learning-based sensing, 3D multi-object tracking, and a sensing--communication closed loop, all reproducible with one-command scripts. Key results: orbit physics matches real ISS values; RIS frame-by-frame tracking improves power by \textbf{+89\%} (K=1), while reconfiguration-limited tracking (K=8) loses the gain; a sensing-aided closed loop achieves \textbf{+309\%} communication gain (97.6\% of the ideal oracle); wideband HRRP classification reaches \textbf{0.80} (9-class); 3D multi-object tracking of 10 targets achieves 0.60 detection recall. A systematic comparison with classical baselines (2D-CFAR, MUSIC) on identical test sets uncovers two transferable findings: (i) a feature-construction defect -- centroid-relative range-profile delays silently discard absolute target position, collapsing ML localization to a class prior (2D RMSE 22.6 m vs 12.1 m with absolute-range features); (ii) a far-field angle-resolution wall -- at ~695 km slant range, the 80 m ROI subtends only 0.0066$^\circ$, far below an 8-element ULA resolution (~14$^\circ$), so mono-static angle-based cross-range localization is physically unavailable. All numbers are reproducible from the repository with fixed seeds.

\textbf{Keywords}: ISAC, RIS, non-terrestrial networks, LEO satellite, diffusion models, CFAR, MUSIC


\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}

Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) unifies wireless communication and radar-like sensing in a single system and is a key enabler of 6G [1, 2]. Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) extend coverage and enhance both communication and sensing at low hardware cost [3, 4]. Non-terrestrial networks (NTN), in particular LEO constellations, are being integrated into 5G-Advanced/6G, and combining ISAC with NTN ("space ISAC") is of growing interest for space situational awareness and terrestrial monitoring [5, 6, 7].

Most published work remains at the level of analytical studies or link-level simulations with simplified geometry. Mature open-source link/PHY platforms (e.g., Sionna [8], MATLAB 5G/NTN toolboxes) exist but do not combine real orbit dynamics, dynamic RIS optimization, learning-based sensing, and closed-loop demonstration in one system. This report accompanies an engineering system that does; it makes no claim of algorithmic novelty over any single component. Its contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A physics-grounded, reproducible space ISAC pipeline} from real TLE orbit data to closed-loop demo, with one-command verification and CI.
\item \textbf{Dynamic RIS phase tracking} for LEO overpasses with an explicit quantification of the "reconfiguration rate vs channel coherence time" trade-off.
\item \textbf{A learning-based sensing layer} (classification + localization on wideband HRRP features) and \textbf{3D multi-object tracking}.
\item \textbf{A systematic classical-baseline comparison} (2D-CFAR + MUSIC vs ML) on identical test sets, surfacing a feature-construction defect and a quantified far-field angle wall.
\item \textbf{An SDR data interface} (IQ format + ingest pipeline) easing the transition to hardware.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System and Signal Model}

\subsection{Scenario Geometry}

A LEO satellite acts as the base station (BS), performs mono-static sensing of a ground region of interest (ROI), and serves a ground user equipment (UE) station. An RIS panel can be \textbf{spaceborne} (~10 m) or \textbf{ground} (~1 m, near UE); modes \texttt{none}/\texttt{sat}/\texttt{ground} are compared. Real TLE ephemerides (ISS NORAD 25544, Starlink) are propagated with SGP4 [9]; ECI$\rightarrow$ECEF conversion includes GMST and Earth-rotation velocity corrections. The overpass window is searched over 48 h; frames are sampled at 1 s near window center where elevation > 20$^\circ$.

Defaults: 30 GHz carrier ($\lambda$ = 1 cm); ISS altitude ~420 km; ROI 80 m $\times$ 80 m at (30$^\circ$N, 120$^\circ$E); UE at (30$^\circ$N, 119.5$^\circ$E); BS--ROI slant $\approx$ 695 km; Doppler -611\ldots+611 kHz; SNR 20 dB.

\textbf{Physics verification} (\texttt{verify\_sat.py}): ISS altitude 418.3 km, velocity 7.66 km/s, period 92.9 min, Doppler S-curve, channel equations -- consistent with published values.

\subsection{Signal Model}

Five propagation paths: (1) BS$\rightarrow$ROI direct illumination, (2) ROI$\rightarrow$UE direct scatter, (3) BS$\rightarrow$UE direct leakage, (4) BS$\rightarrow$RIS$\rightarrow$ROI reflected illumination, (5a) BS$\rightarrow$RIS$\rightarrow$UE and (5b) ROI$\rightarrow$RIS$\rightarrow$UE forwarded paths. For voxel scatterers with complex amplitudes a\_i and round-trip delays $\tau$\_i, the baseband frequency response is

\begin{equation}
H(f) = \sum_i a_i exp(-j2\pi f \tau_i),
\end{equation}

and the wideband range profile (HRRP) is the inverse FFT of H(f) over K = 512 subcarriers at 1 GHz bandwidth (range resolution $\approx$ 0.15 m), plus AWGN. The ISAR sequence extends this over M = 32 frames while the target rotates, synthesizing cross-range resolution. Two delay conventions are used:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{centroid-relative} (\texttt{center='centroid'}): delays relative to the voxel centroid -- shape/pose features, position-independent (classic HRRP);
\item \textbf{ROI-center-relative} (\texttt{center='roi'}): delays retain absolute target position within the ROI (needed for localization); a differential-delay formulation ($\tau$ = 1.1·d\_proj/c, combining the 0.1 BS-side and 1.0 UE-side projection coefficients) avoids K-bin wrap-around.
\end{itemize}

As shown in Section 5, using the centroid-relative convention for localization silently discards position information.

\subsection{RIS Model and Phase Optimization}

The RIS has N unit-modulus phase elements. Phase-aligned configuration maximizes coherent combination of RIS-assisted and direct paths at the UE. Because the satellite moves at ~7.5 km/s, the optimal phase pattern changes over the channel coherence time. We compare:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Frame-by-frame tracking} (K=1): recompute phases every frame -- power \textbf{+89.0\%} vs random (seed-fixed, reproducible);
\item \textbf{Segmented tracking} (K=2/4/8): reconfiguration limited to every K-th frame -- K=2: +60.0\%, K=4: +36.6\%, K=8: \textbf{-41.5\%} (stale phases can even hurt). This quantitatively illustrates the reconfiguration-rate vs coherence-time trade-off.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Target Models}

Ground targets are 16$^3$ voxel templates (5 m voxels; 80 m ROI) with 9 classes: car, UAV, building, tank, tower, cubesat, bicycle, pedestrian, train. Each sample is placed at a random position with a random pose (rotation about the vertical axis), modulating the scattering-center distribution. Isotropic scattering is assumed (no RCS angular dependence, polarization, or occlusion modeled).


\section{Dynamic RIS Tracking and the Sensing--Communication Closed Loop}

\subsection{Sensing Layer}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Diffusion-based 3D reconstruction} (\texttt{train\_sat.py}): conditional latent diffusion (PointVAE encoder + DiT-style denoiser + CFG) reconstructs the ROI point cloud; evaluated with Chamfer Distance (CD), F-Score, voxel IoU. Training-scale results: CD 0.137 (sat) / 0.169 (ground) vs 0.233 (no RIS).
\item \textbf{Classification + localization} (\texttt{train\_sensing*.py}): MLP over wideband HRRP (K = 512) outputs 9-class labels and normalized 2D position within the ROI; CPU real-time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Communication Layer and Closed Loop}

Given sensing output, the IRS phase pattern is configured toward the sensed target. Received power comparison (reproducible, seed-fixed): random phase 1.00$\times$, sensing-aided \textbf{+309.4\%}, ideal oracle +319.3\% (closed-loop efficiency \textbf{97.6\%}). Multi-target closed loop: detection 1/2 in the current 5-class setting, IRS pointing gain \textbf{+443.8\%} (93\% of oracle). A robustness observation: even when classification is wrong, coarse localization captures most of the communication gain.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Demo}

The pipeline is packaged as \texttt{run\_demo.sh} plus a single-file HTML player and GIF animation; a Colab notebook gives a 60-second zero-setup experience.


\section{Wideband/ISAR Sensing and 3D Multi-Object Tracking}

\subsection{Narrowband $\rightarrow$ Wideband $\rightarrow$ ISAR}

Classification accuracy progression (early experiments, 6-class templates; reported for reference): narrowband 0.383 $\rightarrow$ wideband HRRP 0.867 $\rightarrow$ ISAR sequence 0.933. With the current 9-class templates, wideband HRRP classification is \textbf{0.80} (reproducible, \texttt{train\_sensing.py --wideband}, seed-fixed). The progression quantifies the information added by range resolution and by synthetic aperture over rotation.

\subsection{3D Multi-Object Tracking (MOT)}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scene data} (\texttt{mot\_data.py}): 10 moving targets of 5 classes (car, drone, bicycle, pedestrian, train), trajectories straight/accelerating/turning, rendered into per-frame 5-path channel data and range profiles with Doppler.
\item \textbf{Detector} (\texttt{train\_detect.py}): CNN over range profiles $\rightarrow$ (class, position), CPU-trainable.
\item \textbf{Tracker} (\texttt{mot\_tracker.py}): Hungarian association with constant-velocity prediction, ID maintenance, class majority voting, $\alpha$-$\beta$ smoothing.
\item \textbf{3D output} (\texttt{demo\_mot\_html.py}): interactive Plotly HTML with full 3D trajectories.
\end{itemize}

Reproducible results (seed-fixed): detection recall \textbf{0.60} (10 targets), trajectory class accuracy \textbf{0.73}. A physical constraint is enforced: ground targets have z locked to 0 (mono-static range profiles carry weak height information); only aerial targets (drone) have free z.

\subsection{SDR Data Interface}

An IQ format (\texttt{sdr\_io.py}) and ingest pipeline (\texttt{sdr\_ingest.py}) convert time-domain IQ via FFT to range profiles (fidelity \textbf{0.998} vs simulation reference) and feed the sensing layer. Hardware-ready (RTL-SDR/USRP) without changing downstream processing.


\section{Classical Baselines and Physical/Engineering Findings}

Classical radar baselines (\texttt{baseline\_classic.py}) evaluated on the same fixed test set (60 samples, SNR 20 dB, seed-fixed):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{2D-CA-CFAR} (P\_fa = 10-$^{4}$, convolution-vectorized) on the range--Doppler map (slow-time FFT of the ISAR sequence);
\item \textbf{1D-CFAR} on the absolute-range profile with regression-calibrated bin$\rightarrow$meters mapping (20-sample calibration set, R$^2$ $\approx$ 0.83);
\item \textbf{MUSIC} with an 8-element ULA ($\lambda$/2, 64 snapshots). \textit{Honesty note: MUSIC is validated with synthetically generated point-source snapshots (a·s+n) -- it does not share the same signal stream as CFAR/ML; the 0.017$^\circ$ figure verifies algorithmic self-consistency, not end-to-end sensing accuracy.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Finding 1: Feature-Construction Defect}

The original range-profile function computed delays relative to the voxel centroid, silently discarding the target's absolute position. A controlled single-voxel experiment confirmed this: moving the target across the ROI left the range-profile centroid bin \textit{constant}. Consequently, ML localization trained on centroid-relative features collapses to a class-prior estimate: 2D RMSE \textbf{22.63 m} (centroid-relative) and 21.86 m (aligned shape features) vs \textbf{12.06 m} with absolute-range (\texttt{center='roi'}) features -- a ~2$\times$ gap.

\textbf{Fix and verification}: \texttt{compute\_range\_profile} now supports \texttt{center='roi'} (differential delay); \texttt{SatROIDataset} uses it when \texttt{rp\_align=False}. After retraining: classification 0.80, 2D localization RMSE 12.1 m (LOS 2.3 m), and the closed-loop demo is unaffected (+309.4\%, 97.6\% of oracle). Physics verification and module imports remain green.

\subsection{Finding 2: The Far-Field Angle-Resolution Wall}

At ~695 km slant range, 1 m of cross-range offset subtends $\approx$ 8$\times$10-$^{5}$ degrees; the full 80 m ROI subtends $\approx$ 0.0066$^\circ$. An 8-element ULA at $\lambda$/2 has a Rayleigh resolution of $\approx$ 0.886·$\lambda$/(Nd) $\approx$ \textbf{12.7$^\circ$} (upper-bound estimate; even a finer reading $\lambda$/D $\approx$ 14.3$^\circ$ is orders of magnitude larger). Therefore mono-static angle information cannot localize targets within the ROI: ML cross-range RMSE $\approx$ 11.8 m reflects exactly this wall (its cross-range output is driven by class priors and training statistics, not observable angles). This is a physical geometry bound, not an implementation artifact; improvements require multi-static geometry, long-aperture interferometric/ISAR imaging, or temporal priors (tracking).

\subsection{Reproducibility}

All results are produced by one-command scripts with \textbf{fixed global seeds} (\texttt{torch.manual\_seed} + \texttt{np.random.seed} + \texttt{random.seed(42)} at every entry point; verified: two consecutive runs produce identical outputs):

../../.venv/bin/python verify_tracking.py       # RIS tracking trade-off

A GitHub Actions CI pipeline runs import checks, physics smoke tests, and SDR fidelity on every push. A Colab notebook reproduces the core demo in ~60 s.


\section{Limitations and Honest Discussion}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Absolute attitude estimation is not feasible} in the far-field star--ground setting with simple symmetric templates -- a physical upper bound, not an implementation gap.
\item \textbf{Single-station multi-target classification is limited} by signal mixing in range profiles (detection/localization remain usable).
\item \textbf{Cross-range localization is angle-limited} (Section 5.3): mono-static angle-based cross-range localization is physically unavailable at practical array sizes for ~695 km links.
\item \textbf{Target templates are simple voxel models} (isotropic scattering; no RCS angular dependence/polarization); space-debris/satellite geometry models are planned.
\item \textbf{No over-the-air hardware validation yet}: the SDR interface is verified on simulated IQ; RTL-SDR/USRP capture is the natural next step.
\item \textbf{Atmosphere/ionosphere effects are not modeled} (free-space far-field approximation).
\item \textbf{Evaluation scale is modest} (60--150 test samples, smoke-level training, single seed per experiment; multiple seeds and larger runs are a matter of compute). MOT is evaluated on a single scene.
\item \textbf{Historical values}: some early results (6-class classification progression) were produced before the current 9-class templates; they are reported for reference and are reproducible only from earlier commits.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Conclusion}

We presented an open-source, physics-grounded space ISAC engineering system -- real LEO orbits, dynamic RIS tracking, learning-based sensing, 3D MOT, closed loop, SDR interface -- with fixed-seed reproducibility and CI verification. Two transferable findings emerge: (i) a feature-construction defect (centroid-relative delays discard absolute position) that we fixed and quantified (~2$\times$ localization gap); (ii) a quantitative far-field angle-resolution wall bounding mono-static cross-range localization. The system serves as a practical testbed for space ISAC research; the honest limitation reporting aims to raise the reproducibility bar in this emerging area.
